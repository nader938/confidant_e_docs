Introduction, in Dr. Amir's own words

About 3½ minutes

Recorded by Nader Amir.

Hello

Hello, Dr. Amir here. This is a quick intro to our new app, confidant·e, and I just wanted to talk a little bit about what it is and why I use it.

Why

Most AI writing tools send what you type to a company's server. That is fine for birthday messages. It's not fine for a letter to a doctor, or a note about a client, or a draft about a colleague, or questions you'd rather nobody else see.

confidant·e can do the same work with an AI that runs on your Mac. Your words stay with you. With a local AI, nothing you write or ask leaves the computer. It works offline once you've downloaded the model. You don't need an account, and you choose which model to use.

What you need

What you need is this. For the built-in AI, a Mac with macOS 14 or later, 16 gigabytes of memory, and about two and a half gigabytes of free space to download one of the most basic models.

For better results you will need more memory in your Mac and the free Ollama app. For example, 32 to 64 gigabytes for mid-sized models, and 128 gigabytes for the most powerful ones.

And if you want to use the commercial models like Claude, you would need your own API key. There are many tutorials on how to get your API key with a paid subscription.

Why the commercial models are there

That would be different. That would be using the commercial ones, and everything is sent to their server.

Primarily, the reason that we have the commercial ones here is so you can actually do a side-by-side comparison. You can ask a simple question that is not necessarily confidential, and see how the performance of the two models compares. And that way you can decide if it's okay for you to use it or not.

Getting started

So how would you get started? You would download confidant·e. The first thing you see is a welcome page that goes through the basics.

If no AI is ready on your Mac, the app offers to download a built-in one. You can press Download, and you get a two and a half gigabyte model. The bar at the top will show you the download in progress, and when it finishes, the built-in AI is selected for you.

The built-in model and the welcome page

The built-in model is one called Gemma. It is very competent and will do the most basic tasks.

To see the welcome page after you've started the app, just go to the Help menu and choose Welcome to confidant·e.

Cleaning up and dictating

The rest of the buttons in the app are pretty straightforward. You can clean up your writing, and you can see how the AI cleaned up your text.

There's a feature for you to dictate as opposed to type. Dictation is also something that can be sent to servers. So again, there we have two options. One is Apple dictation, which is the default and works straight away. This again sends your data to Apple. But we can also use something called Whisper, which is a local AI and more accurate, and the data is not shared with anyone.

Asking questions

confidant·e also allows you to ask questions. It doesn't just have to be writing. You might have a question about your finances, or about your health, that you don't necessarily want to share with the commercial AI models. For those you will use the same features as in the writing part of the app. Everything works the same. You can compare answers, and everything else.

Close

I hope this introduction got you started, and that you enjoy using the app.

The problem

Most AI writing tools work the same way. You type, and your words are sent to a company's servers.

For a birthday message, who cares. But what about a letter to your doctor? A note about a client? A question you would not want anyone else to read?

The idea

confidant·e does the same job with an AI that runs on your own Mac.

Proof

I press Clean up, and my rough message comes back corrected. Spelling, grammar, punctuation. My meaning and my tone are left alone.

Choosing your AI, local or online

About 2½ minutes

The menu

This menu lists every AI you can use. They fall into two groups, and the difference is where your text goes.

On this Mac

These run on this Mac. The built-in one comes with the app. The others come from a free app called Ollama, and if you have it installed, its models show up here.

With any of these, nothing you write leaves the computer.

What they need

Local AIs need memory. The built-in one runs on a Mac with sixteen gigabytes. Bigger models give better answers and need more: thirty-two to sixty-four gigabytes for mid-sized ones, and a hundred and twenty-eight is recommended for the most powerful.

On the internet

This one is different. Claude runs on the internet, at a company called Anthropic. It is much stronger. But what you send it leaves your Mac.

Adding a key

To use Claude you need your own API key from Anthropic, which you create at platform dot claude dot com. Anthropic bills you for what you use.

I press Add Claude API key, paste it in, and save. It is kept in the Mac's Keychain, so I only do this once.

Use it for one job

Now I can pick Claude for a job where I want the best result and the text is not sensitive. Then I switch back.

The app never chooses an online AI for you. My rule is simple. Private things, local AI. Everything else, your choice.

Memory

About 2 minutes

The problem

Every new conversation with an AI normally starts from zero. You explain yourself again each time.

Memory fixes that. It is a page of notes that carries over from one conversation to the next.

Open Memory

I press Memory. It is empty, because nothing is remembered yet.

Let the AI write the notes

I press Add this conversation. The AI reads what we just talked about and writes notes: the lasting facts, my preferences, what is still undecided.

Check and edit

And this is the important part. I can read it. This line I do not want kept, so I delete it. This one is slightly wrong, so I fix it.

Nothing is saved until I press Save.

See it work

Now a brand new conversation. I ask a question that only makes sense if it remembers.

And it does. It knows where I am going and when.

The shortcut

You do not have to remember to do this. Whenever you press New conversation, the app offers to update Memory first.

What to know

Two things to know. The notes live on your Mac, and you can change or clear them at any time.

And they are sent with each question to whichever AI you ask. So if you ask Claude, the notes go to Anthropic too.

Comparing AIs

About 3 minutes

Recorded by Nader Amir.

Why compare

Hello again. Comparing AIs.

If you have more than one AI, which one should you use? People have strong opinions. confidant·e lets you find out for your own questions.

Of course, there are different ways of defining good. One is accuracy. Two is complexity. And for the purpose of this app, a third, and probably the most important, is confidentiality. A more competent AI might be a very good one to use in some situations, but it might also be one that you cannot use, for various reasons including privacy.

confidant·e will allow you to actually compare some of the answers, to get an idea of which models you should use.

The same question twice

The simple way is to ask a question. You can pick a question, and then ask it of a different AI by selecting the model.

As soon as you do this, you have two answers side by side. You can then rate each one with stars, so you can later on decide which AI to continue using.

Compare blind

But in this case I can see the names, and I may be biased. So there is Compare blind. I type a question, press Compare blind, and pick the two AIs.

Pick a winner

The answers come back as A and B. I read them and choose the better one.

Only now does the app tell me which AI wrote which.

Ask an AI to judge

I can also ask an AI for a second opinion. It reads the question and both answers, and picks the one that it thinks is actually the better answer.

A question set

One question, of course, reveals very little. So I can save a set of my own questions and run all of them across every AI in one go.

I add a question, tick the AIs, hide the names, and have a judge look at each. Then Run, and I go and make some coffee. When I come back, the AIs have produced multiple answers.

The results

Compare ratings adds it all up for each AI: how often I preferred it, how often the judge preferred it, and its average stars.

Now I know which AI is best for me, on my own questions. If the small local one is good enough, I never need to send text anywhere. Or I know that I need to invest in more hardware, a better Mac with more memory, so I can download a more powerful local AI.
