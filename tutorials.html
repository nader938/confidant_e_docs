Write a private letter to your doctor

You will: turn a rough, hurried message into one you can send, without it leaving your Mac.
Time: 3 minutes
You need: the built-in AI, downloaded.

The situation

You need to write to your doctor about test results. You typed it quickly on your lunch break and it shows. It also contains health details you would not paste into a website.

Steps

  1. Look at the menu labelled Clean up with at the top. Make sure it shows Built-in Gemma 3 4B. That AI runs on your Mac, so nothing you type here is sent anywhere.

  2. Click in Your text on the left and type or paste this:

    hi dr patel, sorry for the late reply i been meaning to write for a while.
i wanted to ask about the results from my last visit becuase the nurse said
they was a bit high but she didnt say what that means, should i be worried?
also i cant make the 14th anymore is there any chance to move it to the
week after.

thanks so much for you're help

  3. Press Clean up.

  4. Watch the line under the boxes. It shows a timer while the AI works. The first run after opening the app takes longer, because the AI is loading into memory.

  5. Read the result in Cleaned up on the right. It should look close to this:

    Hi Dr. Patel,

Sorry for the late reply; I have been meaning to write for a while. I wanted
to ask about the results from my last visit, because the nurse said they were
a bit high but did not say what that means. Should I be worried?

Also, I can no longer make the 14th. Is there any chance of moving it to the
week after?

Thanks so much for your help.

  6. Check it against what you meant. The AI fixed the spelling, grammar and capitals, and split the run-on sentence. It should not have changed what you asked or added anything new. If a word is wrong, click in the right-hand box and fix it.

  7. Press Copy, switch to your email, and paste.

What you just did

You got a clean message without your health details leaving the computer. You can confirm that for yourself: turn Wi-Fi off and do it again. It still works.

Try next

  • Press Use as my text to move the result back to the left, add a sentence, and clean up again.
  • Press Save to file to keep a copy.

Speak your thoughts, send a finished message

You will: dictate a rambling message out loud and turn it into a short, clear one.
Time: 4 minutes
You need: the built-in AI, and a microphone (the one in your Mac is fine).

The situation

You owe a colleague an update. You know what you want to say, but typing it out feels like work. Talking is easier.

Steps

  1. Open the Dictate with menu at the top and choose Whisper base (local, fast). With Whisper, your speech is recognised on your Mac. The first time, it downloads a small model and tells you so in a bar at the top.

  2. Press Dictate. If your Mac asks to use the microphone, allow it.

  3. Say something like this, the way you would actually say it:

    Hi Sam, um, so I looked at the numbers you sent, and I think, well, the March ones look fine but April is off, I think there's a column missing, the one for returns. Can you check that? No rush, end of the week is fine. Oh, and thanks for getting it to me early.

  4. Press Stop dictating. Your words are in Your text, with the ums and the restarts.

  5. Press Clean up. The result should read something like:

    Hi Sam,

I looked at the numbers you sent. The March figures look fine, but April is
off; I think the column for returns is missing. Can you check that? There is
no rush; the end of the week is fine.

Thanks for getting it to me early.

  6. Decide whether it still sounds like you. The AI keeps your meaning and tone, but if it made you more formal than you like, edit the right-hand box directly.

  7. Want to add something? Press Use as my text to move the result to the left, press Dictate again, and say the extra sentence. It is added after what is already there. Then Clean up once more.

  8. Press Copy and paste it wherever it is going.

What you just did

You wrote a message without typing it, and neither your voice nor your words left the Mac.

Good to know

  • Apple dictation in the same menu starts instantly with no download, but your speech may be sent to Apple. Use it for things that are not sensitive.
  • Whisper small is more accurate than Whisper base and a little slower.

Ask a question you would rather keep to yourself

You will: ask an AI about a personal money decision, follow up, and keep the useful part.
Time: 4 minutes
You need: the built-in AI.

The situation

You are wondering whether to pay off a loan early or keep the cash as savings. You would like a clear explanation of how to think about it, and you would rather not type your finances into an online service.

Steps

  1. Press Ask at the top left.

  2. Check that Answer with shows Built-in Gemma 3 4B, or another AI that runs on your Mac.

  3. In the box at the bottom, type:

    I have a car loan at 7% interest with 18,000 left, and 20,000 in savings
earning 4%. What should I think about before deciding whether to pay the
loan off early?

  4. Press Ask. The answer appears above. Expect a list of things to weigh: the difference between the two rates, keeping an emergency fund, and any penalty for early repayment.

  5. Follow up without repeating yourself. The AI remembers the conversation:

    Say I keep 8,000 as an emergency fund and put the rest toward the loan.
Roughly how much interest would that save over two years?

  6. Read the numbers carefully. A small AI can make arithmetic mistakes and state them with confidence. Treat the answer as a way to organise your thinking, then check the figures yourself or with your bank.

  7. Rate the answers with the stars underneath. This costs nothing now and helps later, when you compare AIs.

  8. Press the copy button under the answer you want to keep, and paste it into your notes.

What you just did

You talked through a private decision. With an AI on your Mac, the question, your numbers and the answers stayed on the computer.

Good to know

  • The conversation is still there when you reopen the app. To clear it, press New conversation.
  • An AI is not a financial adviser, a doctor or a lawyer. Use it to prepare better questions for one.

Find out which AI is best for your own questions

You will: test two AIs on questions you actually ask, without knowing which is which, and see a score.
Time: 10 minutes, mostly waiting
You need: at least two AIs. The built-in one plus one Ollama model is enough.

The situation

You have the small built-in AI and a larger one in Ollama. The larger one is slower. Is it better enough to be worth it for the things you ask? Opinions online will not tell you. Your own questions will.

Part 1: one question, blind

  1. Press Ask.

  2. Type a question of the kind you really ask. For example:

    Explain the difference between "affect" and "effect" in two sentences,
with one example of each.

  3. Press Compare blind instead of Ask.

  4. Choose your two AIs as First model and Second model, and press Compare.

  5. Wait while each answers in turn. They appear as Answer A and Answer B. The names are hidden and the order is random, so you cannot tell which is which.

  6. Read both. Press A is better, B is better or About the same.

  7. Now the names appear. Was it the one you expected?

  8. For a second opinion, press Have the selected model judge. The AI chosen in the menu reads the question and both answers, picks one and gives a reason.

Part 2: a set of your own questions

One question proves little. Five or ten start to mean something.

  1. Press Question set.

  2. Add questions one at a time: type each in the box and press Add. Use real ones. For example:

    Rewrite this to sound friendlier: "Your request has been denied."
    What are three questions I should ask before signing a lease?
    Summarise the causes of the First World War in four sentences.

  3. Under Models to ask, tick the AIs to test.

  4. Tick Hide which model wrote which answer, and tick the option to have an AI judge each question.

  5. Press Run. Every AI answers every question, one at a time. This is the moment to make coffee.

  6. When it finishes, go through the questions and pick the better answer for each.

Part 3: read the score

Press Compare ratings. For each AI you see:

  • how often you preferred it,
  • how often the AI judge preferred it,
  • its average star rating.

What you just did

You measured the AIs on your own work. If the small local one wins or ties, you never need anything bigger. If the large one clearly wins, you know the extra memory is buying you something.

Good to know

  • Your questions are saved. Open Question set again next month, when a new model comes out, and press Run.
  • An AI judging its own answer may favour itself. Your own blind picks are the stronger evidence.
  • You can include Claude in a comparison. Its answers are sent to Anthropic, so only use questions you are happy to share.

Keep a project going over several days

You will: work on something across more than one sitting, with the AI remembering what matters.
Time: 5 minutes today, 2 minutes tomorrow
You need: the built-in AI.

The situation

You are planning a talk for a community group. You will think about it today, come back tomorrow, and again next week. You do not want to explain the whole thing from the start every time.

Day 1: start, and save what matters

  1. Press Ask and describe the project:

    I'm giving a 15-minute talk to a neighbourhood association about starting a
community garden. The audience is about 30 people, mostly retired, and
some are sceptical about the cost. Help me outline it.

  2. Ask a follow-up or two until the outline is useful. For example: Make the cost section more concrete.

  3. Press Memory. It is empty the first time.

  4. Press Add this conversation, using …. The AI writes short notes: the talk, the audience, the length, what you have decided, what is still open.

  5. Read the notes. Correct anything wrong and delete anything you do not want kept. Then press Save.

  6. Press Save conversation and save the file somewhere you will find it, such as a folder for the talk. The name already contains today's date.

Day 2: pick it up

  1. Open the app and press Ask. Press New conversation if yesterday's is still showing, and choose Start new without.

  2. Ask something that only makes sense if it remembers:

    Suggest an opening line for the talk.

  3. The answer should already know it is a 15-minute talk about a community garden for a sceptical, mostly retired audience. That came from Memory.

  4. Need the full outline back? Press Open conversation and choose yesterday's file. The whole conversation returns and you can carry on from the last answer. The app also switches back to the AI you were using.

  5. When you finish today, press New conversation. This time, choose Update Memory when asked, check the notes, and save.

What you just did

You used two things together. Memory keeps the short list of what matters, and is given to the AI every time. Saved conversations keep the full detail, for when you need it.

Good to know

  • Memory is plain text. You can type into it yourself: I prefer short answers.
  • Memory is sent to whichever AI you ask. If you switch to Claude, the notes go to Anthropic with your question.
  • When the project is over, open Memory, delete the notes about it, and press Save.

Use an online AI for one job, then go back to private

You will: set up Claude, use it for something that is not sensitive, compare it with your local AI, and switch back.
Time: 5 minutes, plus creating a key
You need: an internet connection and an Anthropic API key. Anthropic charges for usage.

The situation

You are writing a public announcement for a club newsletter. Nothing in it is private, and you want the most polished result you can get. This is a reasonable job for a stronger online AI.

Steps

  1. Get a key. Go to platform.claude.com, sign in, and create an API key. Copy it. Treat it like a password: anyone who has it can spend your money.

  2. In confidant·e, press Add Claude API key at the top right. Read the note in the box: text you send to Claude leaves your Mac. Paste the key and press Save. It is stored in your Mac's Keychain, and the button now reads Claude API key saved.

  3. Type or paste your draft into Your text:

    hey everyone the spring plant sale is on saturday april 12 from 9 to 1 at
the community hall. we need volunteers for setup friday night and for the
tills on the day, let maria know if you can help. bring your own bags!!

  4. Press Clean up with your local AI still selected. Press Copy and paste the result somewhere to compare, such as a note.

  5. Now open the Clean up with menu and choose Claude Opus 5.5 (cloud).

  6. Press Clean up again. Compare the two results. Is Claude's better enough to matter for this kind of text?

  7. Switch back. Open the menu and choose Built-in Gemma 3 4B again. The app remembers your last choice, so if you leave Claude selected, your next piece of writing goes to Anthropic too.

What you just did

You used an online AI on purpose, for text you were happy to share, and returned to a private one. The app never makes that switch for you.

A rule that works

  • Private or about another person: an AI on your Mac.
  • Public anyway, and you want the best: Claude is fine.
  • Not sure: treat it as private.

Good to know

  • To stop using Claude, press Claude API key saved and choose Remove key.
  • If you use Memory, your notes are sent along with questions you ask Claude. Clear or trim them first if they contain something private.
  • For a fair test of whether you need Claude at all, see "Find out which AI is best for your own questions".
